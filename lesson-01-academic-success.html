1. Your learning goals

By the end of this lesson, you will be able to:

  • recognise collocations as meaningful word partnerships;
  • complete and categorise common academic collocations;
  • notice combinations that may result from word-for-word translation;
  • check uncertain combinations against corpus examples in SkELL;
  • use at least six target collocations in a short spoken or written response.
Lexical Approach tip
Learn make progress as one useful chunk—not as two unrelated words. Store the whole phrase with its meaning and an example.

The exercise formats in this lesson adapt principles discussed in Michael Lewis, Implementing the Lexical Approach, especially identifying chunks, matching, completing, categorising, sequencing and collocate deletion. The lesson-specific examples are newly written.

3. Read and notice

At university, students often face a heavy workload and have to meet several deadlines at once. Some try to memorise long lists of words, but this does not always help them make progress in academic writing. A more effective strategy is to take notes in meaningful groups and pay attention to words that regularly occur together. When students conduct research, they need to compare evidence before they draw a conclusion. Reading beyond the course syllabus can broaden their knowledge and help them gain insight into unfamiliar topics. These habits may pose a challenge at first, but learners are more deeply engaged when they can see how a strategy supports their goals.

Choose the most sensible order for the five steps. Enter the letters separated by arrows or spaces.

  2. Set a realistic goal and decide when you will finish.
  3. Read relevant sources and take notes in meaningful chunks.
  4. Compare the evidence and draw a conclusion.
  5. Check your work and submit it before the deadline.

5. SkELL corpus lab: hypothesis → evidence → decision

Use SkELL to check two collocations you are not fully confident about. Do not treat it as a translation tool: your task is to inspect how the phrase appears in real examples.

  1. Predict: before searching, write whether you think the phrase is natural and why.
  2. Search: open SkELL, search the whole phrase, and read several examples. Use Word Sketch if available.
  3. Notice: record what the examples show about meaning, grammar or context.
  4. Decide: confirm, revise or leave your hypothesis uncertain. Do not decide from one example or frequency alone.
  5. Record: save the complete chunk, meaning, one example and your own sentence.

a homework → do homework / complete an assignment; a progress → make progress; a conclusion after conduct → draw/reach a conclusion; heavy knowledge → broad/deep knowledge; a deadline after draw → meet a deadline; a research → conduct/do research.

Task 4 — Categories
Planning and deadlines: meet a deadline, make progress, heavy workload. Research and reasoning: conduct research, draw a conclusion, gain insight into. Engagement and attention: pay attention to, deeply engaged, broaden your knowledge.

Task 5 — Sequence
Suggested order: B → C → A → D → E. A different order may be reasonable if explained, but the auto-check key uses this order.
